# Guidance: industrialisation and engineering change

*Guidance only. Nothing in this release tests or records qualification, release or master-data changes.*

## When to read this

Read this chapter when the option changes a drawing, material, supplier, manufacturing site, process, tool, subassembly, configuration or inspection arrangement. A comparable quote is not engineering approval. The base evidence and revision rules apply ([[base:20-evidence-and-drawings]]). This is an original checklist; it does not claim compliance with any sector standard, replace an engineering assessment or define mandatory tests. The responsible organisation supplies the requirements and qualified approvers.

## Change dossier

Record current and proposed configurations, affected variants, the physical mechanism of the change, the design and process owners and the acceptance evidence required (VDT10). Include the intended site and tooling identity: the same corporate supplier at another site is not automatically a qualified source. Engineering and quality define the validation sequence, sample basis, test duration, conditions and failure criteria. Record open uncertainties; do not invent test equivalence. Separate the prototype order, qualification decision, production release and commercial commitment: each has its own authorised scope.

## The transition is part of the economics

Plan the cutover by lot, revision or effective date. Reconcile open orders, work in process, stock in transit, obsolete or reworkable legacy stock, service spares and mixed-revision risk. Review dual running, ramp scrap, extra inspection, expediting, temporary buffers, training and rollback readiness. Start the benefit after the relevant release and usable delivery ([[30-plan-revisions-and-events]]). A credible cost assessment needs the consequences of schedule delay ([[OPS05]], overview only; no scheduling method is reproduced). Treat make-or-buy as a change of operating boundary, using the base incremental-economics discipline ([[base:40-calculation-and-value]]). No assistant changes production master data under this companion.

## Pilot and gate

Before a pilot, freeze baseline and criteria, name the technical decision owner, and define containment, stop conditions, monitoring and fallback. A failed pilot is evidence and stays on record. A pilot supports only the conditions tested; label untested mix, peak load or environment.

The technical owner records whether the change may proceed within a stated boundary; operations confirms launch readiness; the buyer keeps commercial authority; finance keeps benefit recognition. A missing required approval blocks that action. A drawing or site change after a base decision is decision-critical: record it through the base correction path ([[base:M14]]).
